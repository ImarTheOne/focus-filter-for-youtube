Focus Filter for YouTube

Your feed loads normally. When you open a video, a Gemini model checks it against your rules — from its title, description and tags, or by actually watching it — and anything blocked never gets watched.

Google AI Studio API key

Create a free key at aistudio.google.com/apikey. It is stored only in this browser profile and sent only to Google's API.

Before you paste a key, two things Google's terms make your responsibility.

  • You must be 18 or older. The Gemini API terms require it, and they also forbid using the API in a service aimed at under-18s. This extension is not a parental control and must not be set up on behalf of a child.
  • On Google's free tier the videos you open are not private. Google states that content submitted through an unpaid key is used to improve Google's products and that human reviewers may read, annotate and process it. What gets submitted is the details of a video you opened — title, channel, description and tags — and, if you let Gemini watch videos, the video's link, which Google uses to process its picture and sound. On a paid key Google states it does not use prompts to improve its products.

Model

Google retires model names regularly. If you get a 404, it means the selected model does not exist for your key — load the list and pick a real one. A Flash-Lite tier is plenty for this job: one request covers 15 videos.

How closely to check a video

Gemini is given the video's public link; Google fetches and processes it — this extension never downloads it. Only public videos can be watched: unlisted videos and live streams fall back to the details. A free key can watch about 8 hours of YouTube video a day in total, so a shorter limit lets you check more videos. Each verdict is cached, so a video is only ever watched once.

What counts as productive for you

This text is handed to the model verbatim. The more specific you are, the better it judges.

Never show me this (zero tolerance)

One thing per line, in plain words. A video that contains any of it is blocked completely — even when it is only a small part and the rest is exactly what you want. This beats your category rules and the score.

Content rules

What kind of video you don't want, one rule per line — a full sentence or just a topic. A rule blocks a video when it fits the video as a whole or a substantial part of it, whatever its category or score. A passing mention is not enough; for that, use the list above. When Gemini watches a video it judges these on what is actually in it, which is where they shine.

Category rules

Every video gets one category. Allow and Block are absolute and ignore the score entirely — set Gaming to Block and no gaming video ever reaches you. Judge hands it to the strictness score below.

Strictness

When a video is blocked

Opening a blocked video navigates you straight here instead. The address has to be outside YouTube. Note there is no "watch anyway" button once you set this — you are gone before the page loads.

Pause before every video

Every video and Short stays paused first — also one that was checked before and is fine. The screen guides your breathing: the circle grows while you breathe in and shrinks while you breathe out, and a counter shows how much is left. There is no way to skip it, and the seconds only count while you are looking at the tab. Whether the video is blocked, you learn after the pause. The verse changes every day and comes from a list of over 500 that ships with the extension (World English Bible, public domain). None of this needs an API key or sends anything anywhere.

Strict mode (optional)

For when the filter is too easy to get around. Everything in here takes a way out away, from a little to completely: set it as strict as you want. Switched off, these settings are kept but do nothing.

Making it less strict takes effect after

Once saved, anything that loosens Strict mode waits this long: switching Strict mode or filtering off, a lower hard limit, more "Watch anyway", Insane mode or the home page option off, a shorter delay, and a different API key. A new change restarts the wait. Making it stricter works at once, and a waiting change can be cancelled.

The home page is then judged as it loads, from each video's title and channel, and only the videos under the hard limit disappear; everything else stays. This sends the title, channel, length and view count of every video on your home page to Google, and costs one API call per 15 videos.

times a day

Counted across all your tabs and reset at midnight; 0 means never. Once they are used up, a blocked video only offers the way back, as it does under the hard limit.

Gemini watches every video and Short you open, whatever you picked under How closely to check, and your always-allowed channels are checked too. Until the check is done the video stays paused behind the breathing circle, however long that takes, and there is no skip and no "Watch anyway". A video that cannot be checked stays blocked. A blocked video offers a button straight back to the YouTube home page. Needs an API key, and a free key only covers about 8 hours of watched video a day.

Also filter the feed (optional)

Off by default: your home page loads completely untouched and nothing is sent to Google until you actually click a video. Turning these on hides tiles before you click them, but costs an API call for every video that scrolls past.

How blocked videos look

Channels and title words

Ignored while Insane mode is on: it checks every video.

Write a channel as its name, its @handle or its link. Names and title words match whole words, ignoring case and accents: Vox finds "Vox" and "Vox Media" but not "Voxel". End a word with * to cover its endings: react* also finds "reacts" and "reaction". An @handle or a link means exactly that one channel. Blocked beats allowed, and these lists beat every other rule on this page. They are matched here in the browser and work without an API key; a video they decide is never sent to Google.

Saving reloads your open YouTube tabs.
