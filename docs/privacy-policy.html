Privacy Policy — Focus Filter for YouTube

Last updated: 21 November 2026

This add-on is designed so that as little as possible leaves your browser. The developer operates no server and receives no data from you of any kind.

Who is responsible

Focus Filter for YouTube is an independent open-source project developed by a private individual in Switzerland. Contact: kontoonlyne@gmail.com

Because the add-on has no backend, the developer never receives, stores or processes your personal data. The only outbound request the add-on makes goes from your browser to Google, authenticated with your own API key — that is a relationship directly between you and Google, under Google's terms.

You must be 18 or older

Google's Gemini API terms require every API user to be 18 years of age or older, and prohibit using the API as part of a service directed towards, or likely to be accessed by, individuals under 18.

By supplying a key and using this add-on you confirm you are 18 or older. This add-on is not a parental control, must not be marketed or configured as one, and must not be set up on a child's behalf.

What is sent, and to whom

When you open a YouTube video, the add-on sends the video's details to the Google Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) so the model can judge it against your rules. The details are the video's title, channel name, description (up to the first 1,500 characters), tags, YouTube category and length, all read from the page you opened. Your goals text and your two lists of rules (what you never want to see, and your content rules) are sent with them, because they are the question the model is answering. The request is authenticated with your own Google AI Studio API key, which you supply.

If you let Gemini watch videos

The settings page offers three levels of checking. Standard sends only the details above. Smart, the default, and Thorough may also send the video's public link (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…). Google then fetches that video from YouTube itself and processes its pictures and sound to judge what is actually in it. The add-on never downloads, records or uploads the video. It hands Google a link, and Google does the fetching. Only public videos can be watched this way.

Google's servers make that request to YouTube, not the add-on, and it is covered by Google's terms, not by anything the developer controls.

If you filter the feed

Feed filtering is off by default. It is on when you tick a page under Also filter the feed (home page, sidebar, search results, subscriptions, Shorts), or tick Also remove those videos from the YouTube home page under the hard limit. Then, for each video tile on that page, the add-on sends its title, channel name, length, the line showing its views and age, and whether it is a Short, up to 15 tiles per request, so the model can judge it before you click it. No description, tags or link are sent for a tile.

These are the only network requests the add-on makes. There is no analytics, no telemetry, no error reporting and no server operated by the developer. Your data is never sent to the developer, because there is nowhere for it to be sent.

Important: what Google does with it

Google's terms (version effective 23 March 2026) distinguish between unpaid and paid use, and treat Europe differently:

  • Paid use (a key from a Google Cloud project with billing turned on) — Google states that it does not use your prompts or the responses to improve its products. It processes them under its Data Processing Addendum, as a processor, and logs them for a limited period solely to detect and prevent abuse.
  • Unpaid (free) use, if you are in the EEA, Switzerland or the United Kingdom — Google states that the paid-use rules above apply to you too, even though the key is free.
  • Unpaid (free) use everywhere else — Google states that it uses the content you submit and the responses to "provide, improve, and develop" its products, and that human reviewers may read, annotate, and process your input and output. Google says it disconnects the data from your Google Account, API key and Cloud project before reviewers see it.

What is submitted is the details of a video you chose to open, and in Smart or Thorough mode possibly its link, plus your goals and your rules. With feed filtering on, it is also the titles and channels of the videos on the pages you picked. On a free key outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK you should assume that this information is kept by Google and may be read by a person. If that matters to you, use a paid key, or restrict the add-on to the channel and title-word lists, which never leave your browser.

Google states that it may store the data transiently or cache it in any country where Google or its agents have facilities, so it can be processed outside Switzerland and the EEA.

The developer has no influence over any of this and no visibility into it. Google's handling is governed solely by Google's own terms, which you accept with Google when you create your key: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms

What is never sent

  • Your browsing history as such — no list of pages you have visited is ever compiled, read or transmitted. What is sent is the details of a video at the moment you open it, and with feed filtering on the tiles of the pages you picked, as described above
  • Your Google, YouTube or any other account details
  • Cookies, watch history, or anything identifying you — apart from your API key itself, which tells Google whose request it is
  • The video itself — the add-on never downloads, captures or uploads video or audio. (In Smart or Thorough mode Google fetches a public video from YouTube by its link; see above.)
  • Page contents beyond the details listed above

Choose Standard on the settings page if you never want a video's link sent.

Videos decided by your channel lists or your blocked title words are matched inside your browser and are never sent anywhere at all. The pause before a video and the Bible verse of the day send nothing either: the verses ship with the add-on.

What is stored, and where

Everything is stored locally in your browser profile using the WebExtension storage API. Only your API key ever leaves the browser, and only to Google:

  • Your API key. Stored locally. It is sent to Google with each request, as the x-goog-api-key header — that is what authenticates your request against your Google account. It is never sent anywhere else, and never to the developer.
  • Your settings (category rules, both lists of rules, redirect address, channel and title-word lists, goals text)
  • A cache of previous verdicts (video ID, category, score, a short reason and, for watched videos, a one-paragraph summary of what is in them), kept for 30 days by default and capped at 5,000 entries
  • In Strict mode with a daily limit, how many times you used "Watch anyway" today: a count and the date, nothing about which videos
  • In Strict mode, a change that makes it less strict while it waits for the delay you chose, with the time it applies
  • Counters shown in the toolbar popup (videos allowed, blocked and watched, API calls, errors) and the last error message from Google's API with its time

Removing the add-on deletes all of it. You can clear the cache at any time from the settings page.

Limited Use

The use of information received through this extension adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. The data described above is used for one purpose only: judging the videos you open against your own rules. It is not sold, not used for advertising, not used to determine creditworthiness, and not transferred to anyone except Google's Gemini API, which performs that judging under your own key. The developer receives none of it, so no one on the developer's side can read it.

Your rights

Switzerland (FADP / DSG)

The revised Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP), in force since 1 September 2023, gives you a right of access to personal data held about you. The developer holds none — there is no server, no database and no account system, so there is nothing to disclose, correct or delete beyond the data sitting in your own browser profile, which you control and can erase by removing the add-on.

For the data you send to Google under your own API key, your relationship is with Google, under Google's terms. Exercise those rights with Google, through your Google account.

EU / EEA and UK (GDPR)

The same applies. The developer is not a controller or processor of your personal data, because none is received. For the API requests your key authenticates, Google acts as your processor on paid use and, under its terms, on all use in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK; on unpaid use elsewhere it also uses the data for its own purposes, as described above.
